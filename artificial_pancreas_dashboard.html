What is an Artificial Pancreas?
An Artificial Pancreas (AP) system automatically regulates blood glucose in diabetic patients by continuously reading glucose levels via a Continuous Glucose Monitor (CGM) and computing the appropriate insulin dose via a control algorithm, which is then delivered by an insulin pump — all without manual intervention.

Real-World Applications
Impact of artificial pancreas in diabetes management

The Closed Loop Insulin Delivery System represents a major advancement in the management of Type 1 Diabetes (T1D) and insulin-dependent Type 2 Diabetes. Unlike open-loop systems where patients manually calculate and deliver insulin, the AP system continuously senses, computes, and acts — dramatically reducing cognitive burden and clinical risk.

🤖 Automatic 24/7 regulation
🛡️ Prevents hypoglycaemia
🧠 Reduces cognitive burden
😴 Safe overnight glucose control
📈 Improved HbA1c outcomes
🔔 Alerts for abnormal values
🌐 Remote monitoring support
👶 Safe for pediatric use
🎯
Target Population
Primarily used in Type 1 Diabetes patients, where the pancreas produces no insulin at all. Also applicable in advanced Type 2 Diabetes and ICU patients requiring tight glycaemic control.
📊
Clinical Evidence
Clinical trials show AP systems increase Time in Range (TIR) by 10–20% compared to manual methods, significantly reducing dangerous hypoglycaemic episodes.

Biocontrol System Explanation
Closed-loop feedback theory applied to biology
Closed-Loop Feedback Concept
1
Sensor — CGM reads glucose
The Continuous Glucose Monitor measures blood glucose interstitially, every 1–5 minutes, feeding real values to the controller.
2
Error Calculation
Controller computes: e(t) = Setpoint − Current Glucose. A positive error means glucose is too low; negative means too high.
3
Controller — PID computes dose
PID algorithm calculates insulin rate using proportional (P), integral (I), and derivative (D) components to minimise error and stabilise response.
4
Actuator — Pump delivers insulin
The insulin pump micro-infuses the computed dose subcutaneously, lowering blood glucose back toward setpoint.
5
Feedback — Loop repeats
Updated glucose reading feeds back into step 2, continuously correcting and maintaining stability — this is the closed loop.
Control System Concepts
Plant (Process) Human body / glucose dynamics
Setpoint / Reference 100 mg/dL (euglycaemia)
Error Signal Setpoint − CGM reading
Controller PID / MPC algorithm
Actuator Insulin pump
Feedback CGM sensor output
Disturbances Meals, exercise, stress
🎓
Stability Analysis
A well-tuned PID minimises overshoot (rebound hypoglycaemia) and settling time. The system must be stable — glucose must converge to setpoint without oscillation, achieved by careful selection of Kp, Ki, and Kd gains.

Hardware Components
Physical implementation of the artificial pancreas
🩺
CGM Sensor
Continuous Glucose Monitor (e.g., Dexcom G6). Inserted subcutaneously, reads interstitial glucose every 5 min, transmits via Bluetooth.
🖥️
Microcontroller
Raspberry Pi / Arduino Mega. Runs the PID algorithm, receives CGM data, computes insulin dose, and commands the pump driver.
💊
Insulin Pump
Miniaturised motor-driven syringe pump. Delivers insulin in micro-boluses (0.025–0.1 U) via subcutaneous cannula, controlled by PWM signals.
📶
Wireless Module
Bluetooth LE / Zigbee for CGM→MCU communication. Allows remote monitoring via smartphone app and clinical dashboard.
🔋
Power Supply
LiPo battery pack (3.7V, 2500mAh). Provides ~48 hours of operation per charge, with low-power sleep modes between readings.
📱
User Interface
Companion mobile app or e-ink display. Shows glucose trends, insulin history, alarms for hypo/hyperglycaemia, and manual override.
⚡
Signal Flow Summary
CGM Electrode → Transceiver → MCU ADC → PID Algorithm → PWM Output → Motor Driver → Pump Motor → Insulin Cannula → Patient. Feedback occurs every 5 minutes, with rapid corrections computed in under 100ms by the microcontroller.
